# Morrow Glass Field

Morrow is a fictional ambient listening-scene composer used to test whether translucent material can clarify a reading path. The composition is one continuous atmospheric field, not a collection of equal cards.

## Reader job

Decide whether glass distinguishes orientation, the current active scene, and supporting context across light and dark backgrounds without weakening legibility.

## Material roles

1. **Field Veil** — the shallowest blur and lowest opacity; used for persistent orientation and the tuning strip.
2. **Focus Lens** — the strongest edge light and clearest foreground; used only for the active scene rail.
3. **Context Sheet** — medium blur and opacity; disclosed only for supporting material notes.

No fourth glass elevation is introduced. The scene field itself is opaque CSS scenery, not glass.

## Signature and restraint

Activating the scene sends one edge-light sweep through the Focus Lens. The rest of the field stays still, and reduced-motion mode removes the sweep. One accent controls active edge light. There are no decorative glass shards, tile grids, pictographic icon sets, or device-shaped frames.

## Originality boundary

All product names, copy, and CSS geometry are original. The design borrows only broad material principles: reduction, blur, translucency, fine edge light, generous spacing, and restrained state motion. It does not use third-party marks, proprietary assets, device silhouettes, system-screen anatomy, or copied product-marketing structure.

## Fallbacks

- Browsers with backdrop filtering use blur and saturation for depth.
- `@supports not` replaces translucent layers with near-opaque high-contrast surfaces.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` collapses animation and transition durations and removes the edge sweep.
- Controls are native buttons/selects with visible `:focus-visible` treatment and 44px minimum targets.
